# DiagMLP comparator evidence

## Evidence

**Original paper.** The public arXiv v2 manuscript, *Are GNNs Actually Effective for Multimodal Fault Diagnosis in Microservice Systems?*, is six pages and contains the details below (Sections III–V, Tables I and IV): https://arxiv.org/html/2501.02766v2. Separately, the DOI record verifies publication in IEEE ICWS 2025, pp. 127–129: https://doi.org/10.1109/ICWS67624.2025.00025. These are distinct versions; this account uses the public v2 manuscript, not an independently read proceedings PDF.

**Architecture and inputs (III-A–D, Fig. 4, Eqs. 2–3).** For each node, concatenate metric, log, and trace embeddings with a learned node-position embedding. Apply a single affine layer, layer normalization, ReLU, and dropout (the paper’s “Modal_Fusion_MLP”). Concatenate the resulting representations of all N nodes, then apply the same single-layer MLP form (“Node_Fusion_MLP”) to produce the system representation. The position embedding distinguishes node identity; it is not a service-dependency graph. Thus node order/identity is retained through positional embeddings and concatenation, while graph edges/message passing are absent. The method accepts upstream embeddings from any pipeline; experiments preserve each backbone’s preprocessing/embedding rather than defining one universal encoder. Examples discussed include metric/log time-series encoders, alert-sequence embeddings and trace-derived features (II); the paper says SN/TT preprocessing was reimplemented, while GAIA, D1, D2 use authors’ preprocessed multimodal data (IV-A).

**Datasets (Table I, IV-A).** Counts are instances/windows, not node counts; “#instances” separately counts service instances. SN: 12 instances; 316 train, 78 validation, 169 test; detection and localization. TT: 27; 3,256/813/1,744; detection and localization. GAIA: 10; 128/32/939; classification and localization. D1: 46; 63 train, validation shown as “–”, 147 test; localization. D2: 18; 40 train, “–” validation, 93 test; localization. The prose identifies SN and TT as Eadro datasets (Social Network and Train Tickets).

**Localization (Table IV).** Top-k accuracy; entries are mean ± reported variation where supplied. The column order is Eadro, DiagFusion, TVDiag, CHASE, DeepHunt, DiagMLP. SN/Eadro: Top-1 41.8±14.5% vs DiagMLP 80.2±3.1%; Top-3 61.1±16.0 vs 88.6±2.8; Top-5 71.7±10.8 vs 89.4±3.2. TT/Eadro: 91.1±2.2 vs 98.5±0.8%; 96.5±0.7 vs 99.7±0.6%; 97.7±0.6 vs 99.2±0.6%. Note the public table has a Top-k monotonicity anomaly: TT DiagMLP Top-3 (99.7%) exceeds Top-5 (99.2%). GAIA/TVDiag: Top-1 59.8±0.8% vs DiagMLP 61.0±1.0%; Top-3 80.4±0.5 vs 80.7±0.8%; Top-5 88.5±0.2 vs 87.4±0.8%. D1/DeepHunt: Top-1 79.8±0.4% vs 79.8±1.0%; Top-3 90.6±0.6 vs 90.6±0.5%; Top-5 96.7±0.3 vs 95.8±1.0%. D2/DeepHunt: Top-1 78.9±0.7% vs 79.0±0.5%; Top-3 93.4±0.3 vs 93.0±0.7%; Top-5 94.6±0.0 vs 94.6±0.0%. The table note says unvaried values are directly extracted from cited papers; values with variation derive from 10 independent experiments under cited settings. The “±” is reported variation; the table note does not identify it as a confidence interval. D1’s Table IV Eadro (31.0/44.6/48.4) and DiagFusion (33.3/50.5/64.8) cells are not anomalous: both methods also report localization results.

**Caveats.** Authors explicitly acknowledge concatenation makes Node_Fusion_MLP parameter count scale linearly with node count and may challenge scalability; they suggest DeepSets or Set Transformers (III-D). They also say their datasets are small (dozens of instances), recommend larger datasets (e.g. 1000+ instances), and standardized preprocessing (V). These are limitations, not demonstrations that the method fails at a particular N. Fixed-size all-node concatenation implies an input-width dependency on N and no explicit permutation-invariant graph reasoning; that is an architectural consequence. Failure on changing N, generalization to unseen node layouts, or benefit from graph structure are hypotheses not tested here. The reported results instead show strong localization on the evaluated datasets.

**Table/prose consistency flags.** Table IV’s TT DiagMLP Top-3 exceeds Top-5, an apparent monotonicity inconsistency in the public table; values are preserved as printed, not silently corrected. In IV-B the authors’ actual sentence says “D1 Top-1: 79.8 ± 0.4% vs. DeepHunt’s 31.0%.” Table IV instead places 79.8±0.4 under DeepHunt and 79.8±1.0 under DiagMLP; 31.0 is in the Eadro column. Thus the sentence appears to misattribute both the 31.0 baseline column and the ± value, despite the central DiagMLP number being 79.8. GAIA Top-3 is 80.4±0.5 for TVDiag and 80.7±0.8 for DiagMLP: the narrative’s broad “matches or exceeds” phrasing is not a claim of statistical significance; the reported variations overlap.

## Answer

The authors selected DiagMLP as a **controlled ablation comparator**, not as a new end-to-end competing pipeline: replace the GNN modeling block in Eadro, TVDiag, or DeepHunt while holding preprocessing, embeddings, objectives, and training strategy fixed (IV, IV-A). Its purpose is to test whether the GNN contributes beyond multimodal fusion and upstream processing. The manuscript’s hypothesis is that preprocessing—especially traces—and embeddings may already encode useful dependency information. Results in Table IV support the narrower claim that explicit GNN modeling was not necessary to obtain competitive localization on these tested datasets, not that GNNs are universally useless.
